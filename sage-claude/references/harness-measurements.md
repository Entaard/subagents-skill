# Harness measurements

Every dated figure behind `harness.md`. A maintainer reads it, and `/sage-promote` stage three reads it; a run never does — the run sheet carries the rules, and points here wherever a figure used to sit inline.

Each figure carries its date, its population and the dedup rule it was computed under. All of them decay: the corpus grows while a run measures it, old transcripts are pruned, and the harness itself moves between builds. **Re-measure before quoting any number here into a plan whose budget depends on it.**

## Spawning measurements

**`description` is not a handle, 2026-08-28.** A `SendMessage` addressed to a unit's `description` string fails with `No agent named ... is reachable` — both steers of one run, and both resolved once re-addressed by `agentId`.

**`ListAgents` recovers a handle, 2026-08-28 under 2.1.250.** Two calls, one dispatch each — one `completed`, one `running` — each listed under a `Subagents` block by `agentId` with role, state and age, alongside peer sessions; the `description` string appeared in neither listing. Both states are covered by one observation apiece, so treat the recovery path as measured and the `description`-never-appears half as two-for-two rather than as a law. **A 2.1.248 reading recorded the opposite** — that a session's own finished dispatches were absent from `ListAgents`. Whether that was a misreading or a build change is unsettled, and a parent on a 2.1.248-era build should not assume the recovery path.

**2.1.248 background `general-purpose` toolset, re-measured 2026-08-28.** **Harness-attested, from the probe's transcript rather than its report:** `SendMessage` and `Monitor` are **deferred but reachable** — the `deferred_tools_delta` record names both among 30 deferred tools, and a `ToolSearch` for `select:SendMessage,ListAgents` returned `{"matches": ["SendMessage"], "total_deferred_tools": 30}`. So the 2026-08-18 census, which listed both as **absent**, is false under this build: deferred is not absent, and one `ToolSearch` closes the gap. `ListAgents` is in neither list and stays absent. **Self-reported by the probe and NOT attested by any harness record** — carry it as a lead: that its up-front set is `Agent`, `Bash`, `Edit`, `Read`, `Skill`, `ToolSearch`, `Write`, and that `Glob` and `Grep` are absent. `Write` contradicts the 2026-08-18 census and nothing in the transcript settles it either way, so re-probe before relying on it. A unit on that shape creates new files via `Bash` redirection and searches with `grep`/`find` in `Bash` rather than `Glob`/`Grep`. A nested spawn from that toolset succeeds, and its transcripts land in the same session `subagents/` directory (harness sidecar census, `parentAgentId`), so a parent's watchdog keeps covering a nested fleet without any new wiring.

**A `SendMessage` reaches a grandchild, 2026-08-18 — for the finished case.** A unit dispatched by a subagent rather than by the parent is addressed by the agentId in its `subagents/agent-<id>.meta.json` sidecar; a `SendMessage` to that agentId resolved, resumed the unit, and it processed the message: **measured**, a finished grandchild resumed and ACKed. A **running** grandchild draining the message at its next tool round is **not** separately measured — it is the same general mechanism a direct child is documented to use, extended here by inference rather than by its own observation.

**Alt agents listed and unreachable, 2026-09-03.** All three alt agents were listed in the live agent list and all three dispatches failed with HTTP 404 `model_not_found`. The alt models' own vendor listed all three as active and undeprecated, and the cause was neither the vendor nor the account — the machine's owner had switched the lane off. It left no filesystem trace at all.

**A `model` parameter on an alt dispatch, 2026-08-18.** A parent passed `model: haiku` on all three alt dispatches while the brief text in the same call said it overrode nothing. 22.4k spent, three dispatches testing nothing, and no agent caught it — only a deterministic check afterwards did. Measured directly the same day: dispatching `explorer-alt` — whose frontmatter names an outside-family model — with `model: haiku` ran the unit on `claude-haiku-4-5-20251001`, read from `message.model` in its own transcript. The parameter wins; nothing warns.

**`unknown` is an absent measurement, 2026-08-18.** An `explorer-alt` unit that really ran on `gpt-5.6-luna` reported `unknown` in its `MODEL-FAMILY:` line in the same run, because a model that cannot observe its own identity writes `unknown` honestly.

**Spawn-depth limit history, changelog read 2026-08-20.** `CLAUDE_CODE_MAX_SUBAGENT_SPAWN_DEPTH` was fixed at 5 through v2.1.216, dropped to 1 in v2.1.217, and reached its current 3 in v2.1.219; `CLAUDE_CODE_MAX_SUBAGENTS_PER_SESSION` (200, v2.1.212) was removed in v2.1.224. Three moves in three releases is why the run sheet says to check the limits table rather than trust it.

## Dedup distribution

**Sidecar census, 2026-08-18, 212 sidecars.** `model` is **absent on 43%** of them; `parentAgentId` is present only on a nested spawn. This is a wider population than the transcript figures below, which is why it is counted separately rather than called "the same".

**Streaming-partial inflation.** Assistant records are streaming partials: the same `message.id` is written many times — 31 records for 8 distinct ids on one real transcript. Summing raw inflates spend, and the inflation is a **distribution, not a constant**. Measured **2026-08-18** over **197 transcripts** — the watchdog's own readable population: every `<session>/subagents/agent-*.jsonl` under `~/.claude/projects/`, non-recursive, across all projects, `workflows/wf_*/` excluded — min 1.00×, p10 1.67×, p25 1.86×, p50 **2.05×**, p75 2.35×, p90 **2.86×**, p99 **7.31×**, max 8.62×; mean 2.22×, corpus aggregate (all raw ÷ all dedup) **2.26×**.

**These are nearest-rank percentiles, and the rule is part of the figure.** The tail here is three sparse outliers, so a linear-interpolation rule reports p99 as 4.53× on this same data — a 38% difference from one convention, quoted beside a `max` that is an order statistic either way. So a rail built the obvious way fires at roughly half of real spend at the median and under a quarter at the tail — recalling healthy agents constantly, at a rate you cannot predict from any one transcript.

**Quote the population alongside the figures**, always: a recursive sweep drags in the `wf_*/` sidecars of the `Workflow` backend and moves every number, which is precisely how one constant once came to carry two answers. Symlinked duplicates count once — one transcript in this corpus is reachable from two session directories, and double-counting it shifts the aggregate. **Deduplicate by `realpath` before counting, not by path** — the aliases are reachable under two session directories and a plain glob counts both, which moved p50 by 6k in the single session that stamped these figures. **And the corpus grows while a run is measuring it**: this population moved 192 → 197 unique during that same session, because its own units were writing into it. Expect the next measurement to differ by the units you dispatched.

## Signal reliability

Every figure below was measured on **2026-08-18**, over that same 197-transcript population — 188 of them presumed done by the `done` predicate.

| Signal | Reliability | Figures |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `done` | Reliable where it fires; reads shape, never content | Three figures, three different predicates, each named because they are easy to mistake for one: clause (a) alone on the final record fires on **85.3%** (168/197); clause (a) **or** (b) — the predicate the run sheet states — fires on **95.4%** (188/197); the superseded rule, `any` record carrying `stop_reason == "end_turn"`, also reaches **85.3%** on this corpus, because no transcript here carries that value on an earlier record and not on its last. **26 of the 197 carry no record whose `stop_reason` is `end_turn` or `stop_sequence`** — the field is present and simply `null`, which is why "absent" is the wrong word for it — so the superseded test held those finished units as permanently still-running |
| Idle time | Reliable for liveness, noisy as a stall proxy | Per-transcript largest gap: p99 **1137s**, max **1651s** (nearest-rank), so `IDLE_CEIL` (21600s) clears the p99 by ~20x and the largest observed return by ~13x. Both figures move with the corpus — this population no longer contains the 13195s machine-sleep return an earlier measurement recorded, which is exactly the risk of quoting a decayed figure |
| Spend, deduplicated | Reliable | p50 **170k**, p90 **417k**, p99 **818k**, max **851k** — nearest-rank, same population and dedup rule as above |
| Repeated identical tool call | Reliable | `--status` reports the largest count of one identical call across deduplicated records; diagnostic only, nothing on the ladder acts on it |
| Repeated tool errors | Noisy | one expected error is common |

**What `done` cannot see.** The text-only clause is a strict **superset** of the `end_turn` clause, not a looser guess: measured 2026-08-18 over the same 197, every one of the 168 `end_turn` finals ends in a text-only content array, and so do 20 of the 26 transcripts that never reach a completion value. That leaves a residue of **9 (4.6%)** whose final record is a `tool_use` (8) or a truncated `thinking` (1) — a shape identical for a unit running a long tool right now and a unit killed mid-call days ago. Part of any such residue is live rather than dead: the session that measured this had its own units still writing, which is the same corpus-growth caveat above.

## Sampling cost

**One pass per 60 seconds.** Measured on this machine (Apple M2 Pro, 12 cores, 2026-08-18): 0.50s for 18 agents over 28MB and 1.88s for 14 agents over 233MB, so a 60s interval costs about **1% of one core** in the ordinary case and 3% against a quarter-gigabyte of transcript.

**Opening a unit's transcript to check progress, 2026-08-26.** One `TaskOutput`/`Read` on an `.output` file cost **~22k tokens, 8% of that run's occupancy**, for zero usable information, on a fleet whose liveness and per-unit spend were already reported.

## Compaction record

Auto-compaction has fired on this machine. As of 2026-09-04, `grep -rhoa '"trigger":"[a-z_]*"' ~/.claude/projects/ | sort | uniq -c` prints 19 `auto`, 4 `manual`, 1 `refusal`. Fifteen of the nineteen are in one transcript, the parent of sage run `a58bd85c` on 2026-09-03: every assistant record carries `"model":"claude-opus-5"`, its fifteen `compact_boundary` records show `preTokens` between 167,051 and 177,779, so that parent ran in a 200k window (a session setting, not a harness swap) and compacted at about 84 to 89% of it, fifteen times, and closed with its deliverable and a clean `run` line. The model id does not carry the window: `claude-opus-5` appears in that 200k transcript and in 1M transcripts alike, and the `[1m]` suffix never reaches the assistant records. So the reserve between the trigger and the window is measured at 22 to 33k on a 200k window and unknown on 1M; the largest 1M occupancy recorded here, 466,802, never reached a trigger. The 1M window itself measures **1,006,380** tokens (2026-08-18), which is the default `SAGE_WINDOW` in `../bin/sage-watch.sh` and the denominator every pre-2026-09-04 `run` line's percentage used, the 200k session `a58bd85c` included.

**466,802 tokens** of occupancy was reached in real use without a compaction firing — that is a **lower bound on the trigger's location, never its location**, so every slack figure built from it is stated as "at least X, measured to the lower bound," and none is ever measured against the window end.

**The older manual-compaction figures stay as a dated record.** Four events (`preTokens`/`cumulativeDroppedTokens`, 2026-08-18): 92.8%, 93.0%, 93.1%, 95.0% discarded; durations 134,080ms and 131,064ms match the old ~132s figure exactly, the other two ran 114,079ms and 102,446ms.

**Measured parent burn with 7 agents in flight is ~7.7k tokens/min**, which is how remaining headroom converts to minutes.

## Retired figures

- **"No auto-compact has ever fired here" — retired.** It was true on 2026-08-18, when every compaction recorded on this machine carried `trigger:"manual"`, and false by 2026-09-03. Any figure that leaned on it — in particular the reading that "auto-compact discards 99.0% of context in ~132s" was never an observed auto-compact figure — is superseded by `## Compaction record` above.
- **Note-writing cost — retired.** The handoff note these figures measured no longer exists as a mechanism. Writing it cost about **5k output tokens**: 4,942 on the single turn that wrote the 2026-08-20 note, emitting 11,585 bytes / 1,658 words. The file on disk was larger — 13,686 bytes / 1,981 words — because a second writer appended its own log to it afterwards, so never read a finished artifact's size as what those tokens bought. That run's own log row records **~8k** for the same file on a different accounting, so one write produced 5–8k depending on what is counted and neither figure is a band.
- **The handover slack derivation — retired with its mechanism.** "From the 302k threshold to the 466,802 lower bound: 164,888 (~165k), at least ~21 minutes at ~7.7k tokens/min" priced the distance from a 30% rung that no longer exists; its two inputs stand above and the arithmetic is not carried.
- **15–30k — retired, never measured.** The figure that line carried until 2026-08-21 was written the day sage was, before the mechanism it priced had ever run, and nothing ever measured it — retired rather than adjusted.

## Model lineup study

**Studied and rejected, re-checked 2026-09-03: `claude-mythos-5` and `claude-mythos-5-1`** — Fable's restricted-availability twins. **The ground for rejecting them is dispatchability, not equivalence.** Neither name is a value the Agent tool's `model` schema accepts here, so no identity probe can run and neither can hold a seat whatever it buys. State that reason and not the other one, because the equivalence is only partial — and it is worded differently in each release, so quote each from its own source and never carry one's words to the other. The Fable 5 page says Mythos 5 "shares Claude Fable 5's specifications and pricing; Claude Fable 5 includes safety classifiers that can decline requests, and Claude Mythos 5 does not." For 5.1 the vendor's launch announcement says instead that the two "are the same model, but with different levels of safeguards" — that sentence is on the announcement, not on any model page — and no 5.1 page repeats the contrast: the What's new page says Fable 5.1 keeps those classifiers, and nothing anywhere says Mythos 5.1 lacks them. Both are real differences in safeguards, and neither changes anything here while the schema cannot select the model. Both stay invitation-only, to approved customers; Mythos 5 is positioned for cybersecurity and biology research, with its cybersecurity access framed as defensive. The older `claude-mythos-preview` is **deprecated**, with the vendor directing migration to `claude-mythos-5` — no lineup consequence, since none of them ever held a tier.

**The step-count rule's evidence is external and thin, and it enters saying so** (calibration: provisional). The 2–3× ratio comes from another harness. The one measurement cited for it is a *composition* statistic — what share of one campaign's subagent turns ran on the cheap tier — carrying no task denominator and no matched arm, so it is equally consistent with the cheap tier simply being dispatched more often. Nothing local covers it either: the band KIs in `../memory/local/` name "any substantial `haiku` run" among the uncovered classes, and this is the first placement rule to touch that class. Read it as a reason to ask the question before placing a multi-step unit on the fast tier, not as a number to compute with — and let the first covered `haiku` run settle it.

**Fable 5.1 identity, 2026-09-03.** The vendor's model page dates Fable 5.1's release 2026-09-01, and a probe on 2026-09-03 resolved `fable` to `claude-fable-5-1` — at Fable 5's own price, window and latency position, with cache reads cut to a quarter of the old rate. The vendor price ratio haiku : sonnet : opus : fable = 1 : 2 : 5 : 10, input and output alike, was re-confirmed exactly against the vendor's own model docs on that date, so no tier moved on price.
