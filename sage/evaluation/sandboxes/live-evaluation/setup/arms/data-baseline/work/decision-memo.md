# North Pier Museum: queue-sign decision

Recommend a small, reversible new-sign pilot for one further exhibit weekend, subject to the museum's ordinary scheduling decision. The current data do **not establish that the new sign improves completion**, and do not justify permanent adoption. This memo makes a recommendation only; it commits no budget, staffing, safety changes or announcement (N5).

Completion means reaching the final exhibit station; rows are disjoint and each visitor appears once (N1). The table uses only rows with known completion counts. Differences are **new minus old in absolute percentage points (pp)**, calculated from pooled counts, not averages of row percentages.

The 95% Wilson intervals with z=1.96 are **69.19%–77.69%** for old (302/410) and **55.26%–65.06%** for new with known outcomes (229/380; M01–M08). These are descriptive binomial intervals for the observed fractions. They do not account for the missing group, nonrandom assignment, or shared session conditions; they are neither causal-effect intervals nor proof that old is better. The aggregate groups may have correlated outcomes within sessions, so nominal binomial coverage is not guaranteed by this collection procedure (N2, N4).

M09 has 20 visitors and an irretrievably missing completion count (N3). There are 400 new-sign visitors in total, but only 380 with observed outcomes; treating 229/400 as the observed completion fraction would silently assume zero completions. Let the missing count be k, any integer from 0 to 20. Then the full new-sign fraction is (229+k)/400 = **57.25%–62.25%**, and its difference from old is **−16.41 to −11.41 pp**. This is a worst-case identification range, not a confidence interval. The missing count can span 5.00 pp of the overall comparison; relative to the observed-outcome difference, it can shift the estimate by −3.01 to +1.99 pp.

The full busy new-sign fraction is (152+k)/310 = 49.03%–55.48%, leaving a busy-block difference of **+6.18 to +12.63 pp**. Week 2 becomes (120+k)/210 = 57.14%–66.67%, with difference **−15.86 to −6.33 pp**. Quiet and week 1 are unchanged. Thus no possible M09 count reverses any table comparison's direction (M01–M09, N3). The script enumerates all 21 possibilities, including every week-by-block comparison: within week 2 busy the new-minus-old difference remains +3.13 to +15.62 pp (M07–M09).

The reversal between overall and within-block results is a composition effect consistent with the staff's assignment procedure: 310/400 = 77.50% of new-sign visitors were in busy sessions versus 70/410 = 17.07% of old-sign visitors (M01–M09, N2). Completion is much lower in busy sessions for either sign. Even within each week and crowd block, the observed new-sign rate is higher: week 1 quiet +5.00 pp and busy +10.00 pp; week 2 quiet +6.00 pp and busy +10.00 pp before M09 (M01–M08). This pattern is promising, but adjustment for crowd labels and week cannot remove uncontrolled visitor mix or staff sign selection (N2, N4). Crowding labels were recorded before opening, which supports their use as pre-session strata, not random assignment (N2).

Only two weeks and aggregate groups are available. Stable staffing and route length remove two reported sources of variation, but visitor mix and crowding were uncontrolled, no visitor-level records exist, and the data do not establish representativeness for another exhibit weekend (N4). The missing export is documented; its completion distribution is unknown, so no missing-at-random assumption is warranted (N3).

For the next weekend, propose 16 fixed-length sessions, arranged as eight matched pairs across day/time and pre-opening quiet/busy labels, with four pairs planned per crowd label. Randomly assign one old and one new session within each pair before visitors arrive; switch signs between sessions and record deviations. Keep staffing and route fixed, define completion identically, and reconcile visitor/completion tallies with a backup at every session close (N1–N4). Analyze visitors under the assigned sign, retaining sessions with deviations. Report counts and block-specific differences, plus an equally weighted average of paired session-rate differences with uncertainty based on session-pair randomization; identify this session-average estimand explicitly. Log actual crowd conditions and incomplete tallies. Stop at the weekend/16-session cap, and report unfilled strata or missing data rather than expanding the study automatically. This small pilot may be imprecise; it is a feasibility and effect-estimation step, not a promised definitive test.

Before starting, ask the museum to accept or revise a provisional worthwhile gain of +5 pp. Evidence that would change the decision toward continued use is a well-executed randomized pilot whose prespecified overall effect is at least +5 pp, with its 95% interval above zero and no consistent crowd-block harm. A negative effect with an interval wholly below zero, or repeated block-specific harm, would favor retaining the old sign. An interval spanning meaningful benefit and harm would leave adoption unresolved; any larger study would be a separate decision. Reliable counts, assignment adherence, and balance determine whether these findings deserve weight. These thresholds are proposed operational criteria, not facts inferred from the present data.

Reproduce from this folder with `python3 analysis.py`; the stdlib-only script writes `summary.json`, including unrounded fractions, denominators, Wilson intervals, every missing-count scenario, row IDs, and SHA-256 hashes of both raw sources.
